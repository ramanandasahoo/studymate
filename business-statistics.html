Business Statistics

Revision notes, important questions, quiz, flashcards and a study timer for Business Statistics.

Quick Revision

Definition of Statistics

Statistics is the science of collecting, organizing, presenting, analyzing and interpreting numerical data in order to make effective decisions.

Primary Data

Primary data is data collected for the first time by the investigator himself, directly from the original source, for a specific purpose.

Secondary Data

Secondary data is data that has already been collected by some other person or agency and is reused by the investigator for his own study.

Graph

A graph is a visual representation of numerical data using lines, bars or curves, which makes comparison and understanding easier.

Diagram

A diagram is a simple pictorial presentation of data using shapes such as bars, rectangles or circles to show facts quickly and clearly.

Pie Chart

A pie chart is a circular diagram divided into sectors, where each sector represents the proportion of a component in the total.

Mean / Average

The mean is the sum of all observations divided by the total number of observations. It is also called the arithmetic average.

Median

The median is the middle value of a series when the data is arranged in ascending or descending order.

Mode

The mode is the value that occurs most frequently in a set of data. A series may have one mode, more than one mode, or no mode.

Tabulation of Data

Tabulation is the systematic arrangement of collected data in rows and columns in the form of a table, so that it can be easily read and analyzed.

Important 5-Mark Questions

1. Explain the functions of statistics.

1. Collection of data – gathering facts and figures.
2. Organization of data – arranging data in a systematic order.
3. Presentation of data – showing data through tables, graphs and diagrams.
4. Analysis of data – calculating averages, dispersion and relationships.
5. Interpretation of data – drawing conclusions.
6. Forecasting – helping to predict future trends.
7. Policy formulation – helping government and business take decisions.

2. Explain the limitations of statistics.

1. Deals only with quantitative data.
2. Does not study individuals – only aggregates.
3. Results are true only on average.
4. Can be misused by wrong presentation.
5. Requires expert knowledge to interpret.
6. Uniformity and homogeneity of data are necessary.
7. Conclusions may be misleading if data is inaccurate.

3. Explain the characteristics of statistics.

1. It is a science as well as an art.
2. It deals with aggregates of facts, not individuals.
3. Data must be numerically expressed.
4. Data must be collected systematically.
5. Data must be comparable.
6. Results are affected by a large number of causes.
7. It helps in drawing rational conclusions.

4. Explain the scope of statistics.

1. Economics – measuring national income, demand, supply.
2. Business – sales, production, marketing research.
3. Commerce – accounting, banking, insurance.
4. Industry – quality control, production planning.
5. Government – taxation, population, planning.
6. Social sciences – sociology, psychology, education.
7. Science and research – experiments and testing.

5. Explain different methods of statistics.

1. Collection of data – primary and secondary methods.
2. Organization – classification and tabulation.
3. Presentation – graphs, diagrams, tables.
4. Analysis – measures of central tendency, dispersion, correlation, regression.
5. Interpretation – meaning and conclusion of results.
6. Forecasting – estimating future values using trends.

6. Differentiate between primary data and secondary data.

Primary data:
– Collected for the first time.
– Collected by the investigator himself.
– Original in nature.
– More reliable but costly and time-consuming.

Secondary data:
– Already collected by someone else.
– Obtained from published or unpublished sources.
– Not original in nature.
– Less costly, quicker, but may be less reliable.

7. Explain the characteristics of a good average.

1. It should be easy to understand and calculate.
2. It should be based on all observations.
3. It should be rigidly defined by a formula.
4. It should be suitable for further algebraic treatment.
5. It should not be much affected by extreme values.
6. It should be capable of being used for comparison.
7. It should be stable from sample to sample.

8. Explain the merits and demerits of arithmetic mean.

Merits:
– Easy to understand and calculate.
– Based on all observations.
– Rigidly defined.
– Suitable for algebraic treatment.

Demerits:
– Affected by extreme values.
– Cannot be calculated for open-end classes.
– Cannot be located by inspection.
– May give misleading results for skewed data.

9. Explain the merits and demerits of median.

Merits:
– Easy to understand.
– Not affected by extreme values.
– Can be used for open-end classes.
– Can be located by inspection.

Demerits:
– Not based on all observations.
– Not suitable for algebraic treatment.
– Requires data to be arranged in order.
– Less stable for small samples.

10. Explain the merits and demerits of mode.

Merits:
– Easy to understand.
– Not affected by extreme values.
– Useful for qualitative data.
– Can be found by inspection.

Demerits:
– Not based on all observations.
– Not suitable for algebraic treatment.
– A series may have more than one mode or no mode.
– Ill-defined in some cases.

11. Explain the methods of classification of data.

1. Geographical classification – based on place or area.
2. Chronological classification – based on time.
3. Qualitative classification – based on attributes such as gender, religion, occupation.
4. Quantitative classification – based on numerical values such as marks, income, age.
5. Discrete and continuous classification – based on the nature of the variable.
