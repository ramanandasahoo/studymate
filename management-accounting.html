Management Accounting

Diploma in Modern Office Management — Quick Revision, Important Questions, Quiz & Flashcards

Quick Revision

1. Meaning of Management Accounting

Management accounting is the process of collecting, analysing and presenting accounting information to the management for planning, controlling and decision-making.

2. Objectives

  • To supply useful information to management.
  • To help in planning and forecasting.
  • To help in controlling performance.
  • To help in decision-making.
  • To help in fixing prices and preparing budgets.

3. Functions

  • Recording of transactions
  • Analysing and interpreting data
  • Reporting to management
  • Budgeting and forecasting
  • Cost control and cost reduction

4. Tools and Techniques

  • Ratio Analysis
  • Fund Flow Statement
  • Cash Flow Statement
  • Marginal Costing
  • Break-Even Analysis
  • Budgetary Control
  • Standard Costing

5. Important Formulas

  • Current Ratio = Current Assets ÷ Current Liabilities
  • Quick Ratio = Quick Assets ÷ Current Liabilities
  • Gross Profit Ratio = (Gross Profit ÷ Net Sales) × 100
  • Net Profit Ratio = (Net Profit ÷ Net Sales) × 100
  • Contribution = Sales − Variable Cost
  • Break-Even Point (units) = Fixed Cost ÷ Contribution per unit
  • Margin of Safety = Actual Sales − Break-Even Sales
  • P/V Ratio = (Contribution ÷ Sales) × 100

Important 2-Mark Questions

Q1. Define management accounting.

Management accounting is the presentation of accounting information to management for planning, controlling and decision-making.

Q2. Who are the main users of management accounting?

Internal users, mainly the management of the organisation.

Q3. State any two objectives of management accounting.

(i) To help in planning and forecasting. (ii) To help in decision-making.

Q4. What is ratio analysis?

It is the technique of calculating and interpreting relationships between two accounting figures.

Q5. What is the ideal current ratio?

2 : 1.

Q6. What is a cash flow statement?

A statement showing the inflow and outflow of cash during a particular period.

Q7. What is contribution?

Contribution = Sales − Variable Cost.

Q8. What is break-even point?

The level of sales at which there is neither profit nor loss.

Q9. What is budgetary control?

It is the establishment of budgets and comparison of actual performance with the budget.

Q10. What is standard costing?

A technique of cost control in which actual cost is compared with a predetermined standard cost.

Important 5-Mark Questions

Q1. Explain the objectives of management accounting.

1. Planning: Helps management in setting targets and preparing budgets.
2. Controlling: Compares actual results with plans and finds deviations.
3. Decision-making: Supplies data for make-or-buy, pricing and product decisions.
4. Reporting: Presents information in a simple and useful form.
5. Cost control: Helps in reducing cost and improving profit.

Q2. State the functions of management accounting.

1. Recording of financial transactions.
2. Analysing and interpreting accounting data.
3. Reporting results to management.
4. Forecasting and budgeting.
5. Cost control and cost reduction.
6. Assisting in decision-making.

Q3. Distinguish between financial accounting and management accounting.

1. Purpose: Financial accounting records past results; management accounting helps future decisions.
2. Users: Financial accounting is for outsiders; management accounting is for internal management.
3. Reporting: Financial accounts are prepared yearly; management reports are prepared as and when required.
4. Rules: Financial accounting follows fixed rules; management accounting has no fixed rules.
5. Nature: Financial accounting is historical; management accounting is future-oriented.

Q4. Explain any five tools of management accounting.

1. Ratio Analysis: Studies relationships between accounting figures.
2. Fund Flow Statement: Shows sources and uses of funds.
3. Cash Flow Statement: Shows inflow and outflow of cash.
4. Marginal Costing: Separates fixed and variable costs for decisions.
5. Budgetary Control: Compares actual performance with budgets.

Q5. What is break-even analysis? State its importance.

Meaning: It is the study of the relationship between cost, volume and profit to find the break-even point.
Importance:
1. Helps to find the no-profit-no-loss point.
2. Helps in fixing selling price.
3. Helps in profit planning.
4. Helps in deciding the margin of safety.
5. Helps in making make-or-buy decisions.

Q6. Explain the advantages of budgetary control.

1. Helps in planning and coordination.
2. Fixes responsibility on departments.
3. Helps in controlling cost.
4. Helps in measuring actual performance.
5. Helps in achieving the targets of the organisation.
